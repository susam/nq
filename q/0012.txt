If the line graph \( L(X) \) of a graph \( X \) has no triangle, what does this imply about \( X \)?

---

\( X \) has no vertices of degree at least \( 3 \).

---

\( X \) has no vertices of degree at least \( 2 \).


---

\( X \) has no vertices of degree at least \( 4 \).

---

\( X \) is a path.

---

\( X \) is a cycle.

---

The line graph \( L(X) \) has a vertex for each edge of \( X \), with
two vertices adjacent if the corresponding edges in \( X \) share a
common vertex.  A triangle in \( L(X) \) corresponds to three edges in
\( X \) that are pairwise adjacent, which happens exactly when those
three edges meet at a single vertex.  Therefore, if \( L(X) \) has no
triangle, the graph \( X \) cannot have any vertex incident to three
or more edges.  Equivalently, every vertex of \( X \) has degree at
most \( 2 \), so \( X \) is a disjoint union of paths and cycles.  No
vertex has degree greater than or equal to \( 3 \).
