If the line graph \( L(X) \) of a graph \( X \) has no triangle, what
does this imply about \( X \)?

---

\( X \) has no vertices of degree at least \( 3 \).

---

\( X \) has no vertices of degree at least \( 2 \).


---

\( X \) does not contain a cycle.

---

\( X \) is a path.

---

\( X \) is a cycle.

---

In the line graph \( L(X) \), each edge of \( X \) becomes a vertex.
Two vertices in \( L(X) \) are adjacent if their corresponding edges
in \( X \) have a common vertex.

Suppose \( L(X) \) has no triangles.  If any vertex in \( X \) belongs
to three or more edges, those edges would correspond to three vertices
that form a triangle in \( L(X) \).  Since \( L(X) \) has no
triangles, \( X \) cannot have a vertex that belongs to three or more
edges.  Therefore every vertex in \( X \) has degree at most \( 2 \),
i.e. no vertex of \( X \) has degree at least \( 3 \).
