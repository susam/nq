<em>The Mother of All Demos </em> was a live computer demonstration
presented on 09 Dec 1968 at the Fall Joint Computer Conference in San
Francisco.  During this 90 minute presentation, a wide range of
groundbreaking concepts were shown that would later become central to
modern computing.

The demonstration introduced ideas such as the computer mouse,
windowed graphical interfaces, hypertext, real time text editing,
video conferencing and collaborative work on networked computers.
These concepts were decades ahead of what was commonly available at
the time and profoundly influenced the future of human computer
interaction.

The presentation was delivered by Douglas Engelbart, a computer
scientist at the Stanford Research Institute, who led the team that
developed the system used in the demonstration.
