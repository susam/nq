The brain of a domestic cat is roughly the size of a walnut.  It is
about 5 cm in length.  It typically has a mass between 25 g and 30 g.
A cat's brain usually has a mass of less than 1% of its total body
mass.  By comparison, the human brain has a mass of about 2% of the
total body mass.

Much of a cat's brain is devoted to processing sensory information and
controlling its movements.  Cats make extensive use of their sight,
hearing and sense of smell, and use their whiskers to feel their
immediate surroundings.  They can also learn by watching other
animals, solve problems and remember things for long periods of time.
