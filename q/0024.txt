In musical notation, what are the British names for whole, half,
quarter and eighth notes, respectively?

---

Semibreve, minim, crotchet and quaver

---

Minim, semibreve, quaver and crotchet

---

Semibreve, crotchet, minim and semiquaver

---

Breve, minim, crotchet and semiquaver

---

Whole note, half note, quarter note and eighth note

---

The British names for whole, half, quarter and eighth notes are
semibreve, minim, crotchet and quaver, respectively.  The table below
shows these and other note values with their American equivalents.  Each
value is half as long as the one above it.

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>British Name</th><th>American Name</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Breve</td><td>Double whole note</td></tr>
<tr><td>Semibreve</td><td>Whole note</td></tr>
<tr><td>Minim</td><td>Half note</td></tr>
<tr><td>Crotchet</td><td>Quarter note</td></tr>
<tr><td>Quaver</td><td>Eighth note</td></tr>
<tr><td>Semiquaver</td><td>Sixteenth note</td></tr>
<tr><td>Demisemiquaver</td><td>Thirty-second note</td></tr>
<tr><td>Hemidemisemiquaver</td><td>Sixty-fourth note</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

Shorter note values are very rare in musical notation.  A 128th note is
called a semihemidemisemiquaver, and a 256th note a
demisemihemidemisemiquaver.
